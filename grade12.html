Review of Derivatives: The Product Rule

The product rule for derivatives is Dₓ(f ● g)(x) = f(x) ● g'(x) + g(x) ● f'(x). We derive this formula from the limit of the difference quotient for (f ● g)(x).

Watch video ↗

Review of Derivatives: The Quotient Rule

The derivative of f(x)/g(x) is equal to [g(x) ● f'(x) - f(x) ● g'(x)] / [g(x)]^2. A common mnemonic to remember the formula is "low de-high minus high de-low, over low-low", where "high" is the function in the numerator f(x) and "low" is the function in the denominator g(x).

Substitution3 videos

Integration by substitution (change of variable)

The theorems that we have previously used in evaluating integrals cannot be used directly for many antiderivatives. The chain rule for differentiation gives us a method for finding the antiderivative if the antiderivative can be expressed as a composite function. This method is called integration by substitution or the #u-substitution method.

Watch video ↗

Examples on integration by substitution (indefinite integrals)

The substitution method of integration is used when the antiderivative can be expressed as a composite function. In this video we take a look at four problems to see how we can identify the expression to assign to the variable u, and then use the method to find the integral of our given expression. When dealing with indefinite integrals using the substitution method, we need to back-substitute and transform our variable back to x from u.

Watch video ↗

Integration by substitution: Examples with definite integrals

We have seen that we can use the substitution method for functions whose antiderivatives can be expressed as a composite function. If we are just looking for an antiderivative, the last step in the solution is to back-substitute from the u-variable back to the x-variable. However if we are evaluating definite integrals, we can skip the back-substitution if we transform the limits of integration (which are values of x) to their corresponding equivalent values in the u-variable. In this discussion we take a look at three exercises involving definite integrals requiring the use of the substitution method.

Integration by Parts3 videos

Integration by parts

The formula for integration by parts is ∫ u dv = uv - ∫ v du. The goal in using this method is to reduce a more complex integral, ∫ u dv, into a less complex integral, ∫ v du. The formula is derived from the product rule of differentiation.

Arc Length3 videos

Measuring the arc length

We can approximate the length of a smooth curve or the arc length by constructing line segments connecting points along the curve and taking the sum of the lengths of these line segments. Using calculus (more specifically, limits), we can find the exact length by taking the limit as the number of line segments (or subintervals) approach infinity. In this video we derive the formula for finding the length of a arc bound by an interval where the function is smooth in the said interval.
